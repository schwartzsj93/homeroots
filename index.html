How to read your home's ancestry

Enter an address. HomeRoots looks up its parcel at Lake County, outlines it on the map, decodes the survey grid it sits in, finds the recorded subdivision and earlier parcels, and stacks the names written on historical maps of that section.

  • Parcel / PIN: today's tax lot. The 10-digit number encodes township, section and quarter-section.
  • Subdivision (plat): the recorded drawing that carved a farm into lots.
  • Section-Township-Range: the 1830s federal survey grid every old map shares.
  • Chain of title: the ordered list of owners, proven by deeds and abstracts.

Use the layer panel on the map to fade the modern lot over the 1870s county map, Rudolph's 1909 map, the 1925 Windes & Marsh map, the 1939 aerial and the county's 1996–2024 parcel history. The three scans are georeferenced to the county's section corners (within about 20 m). Press Adjust placement if you want to fine-tune one. Placements are saved in this browser and can be exported.
